Why one ticket was wrong

Decision 0001 merges on the subject line. Twelve different subjects all say “wrong day”, so everything about dates landed in one ticket. The subject says what the reporter felt. It does not say what broke.

The cause depends on the tenant, not on what intake guessed from the reporter’s browser. The table below groups the same reports by the cause the desk can work out (src/lib/kb/causes.ts). The tagged as column shows how often intake’s guess differs.

The three problems

  1. Parser reads month first. The shared parser reads every date month first, so every day-first tenant gets a flipped month and day. The queued fix covers this one.
  2. Booking-v3 hint. Tenants on the booking-v3 form print MM/DD/YYYY under the date field, so their people type month first through the form. This is a different bug with a different fix: the form, not the parser. The queued fix does not cover it.
  3. One-day shift. A few reports describe a date that is one day off, not a month flip: June only, an invite that arrived before the booking, and one meeting held twice across time zones. A flip cannot explain these. No cause is known yet; time zones are a lead.
Known is true for the first problem only. The other two have no ticket of their own until they are filed apart.

Lessons

  • Merging on subject counts a feeling once. Count a cause once.
  • Look the tenant up before you merge. Intake’s tag is a guess.
  • Do not edit an accepted decision. Write a new record that replaces 0001.
